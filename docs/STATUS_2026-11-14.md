## 1. What Field Studio is now, and what changed

### 1.1 What it is
Field Studio is an experimental, open-source environment for modeling and sculpting with implicit geometry. It combines
parameterized forms with locally sampled edits (a sparse, multi-level brick lattice, finest level 0.25 mm), allowing
tools to change geometry through field operations and spatial deformation. Edits are commands that are logged,
undoable and replayable to the same state hash. Its viewport renders the resulting fields directly on the graphics card
(a CPU tracer remains as the reference and the fallback), connecting shape changes and material appearance in the
working view. There is no triangle, quad or tetrahedron anywhere in the model, the tools, the renderer or the tests.

The interaction design emphasizes understandable actions and visible scope. Tools are organized around what the user
wants to do, with related controls presented consistently and tool-specific behavior expressed in meaningful terms.
Surface following, directional editing, reach, protection and contact describe how an action engages the model.

The architecture brings several mechanisms together: sparse field storage, geometric combinations, a shared warp
engine, procedural material definitions, progressive rendering on the card, and recoverable editing history. These
mechanisms serve a practical purpose: helping a person create, inspect, refine and arrange forms without continually
stepping out of the modeling process to manage its representation.

Development proceeds through actual creative work as well as targeted engineering checks. A usable baseline enables
hands-on sessions; the results expose the next problems to solve. The application is under active development, with
demonstrated hands-on sculpting use (section 1.2) and continuing work on tool behavior, material controls, numerical
consistency and stability. Animation and simulation are research directions (section 4), not capabilities of the
window in this release. The first release describes the store, its writer, the brushes, contact, posing, cloth, hair
and light in detail; this review covers what has happened since.

### 1.2 What changed, in one paragraph
On 2026-11-12 a head could not be blocked out with the tools, and those tools were derivatives of a mesh-based system
translated over. Decided on the evening of 2026-11-13: a new toolset of the project's own (Shape, Swell / Settle,
Flatten, Pencil, Cut, Merge, Connect, Hollow as a property of a shape, Place on surface, and the basics). A design lane
turned the definitions into five documents (the toolset, the interface, the skeleton and rig, open libraries and
patents, and the tier ladder of acceptance scenarios), approved for building at about 00:23 PT on 2026-11-14.
Overnight, four building lanes put most of the object tools and three of the new brushes into the real window, fixed the
cause of the terraces and fragments seen in hands-on sculpting, and wired a first materials engine (stone and brick as
structure in the field). At 08:23 PT a timed end-to-end session passed in the real window on the card. At about
11:02 PT the tool was tested by hand: a head could almost be roughed in, and the forming tool behaved as intended;
the blocker was that the radius, strength and softness of the move brush could not be changed quickly. That test is
the truest measure in this review, and the work since has gone to what it found.

- The milestone's CPU run is now a landing gate (section 6) and has passed on at least four later code versions

### 2.2 The field stays a distance field under editing
**The problem.** An investigation of the 2026-11-13 hands-on sculpting session photographed terraces,
walls and fragments appearing as strokes piled up. Reproduced on a deterministic regression that reads the tracer's own
values on a fresh sphere, the stored field stopped being a distance as strokes accumulated: its largest gradient (a
Lipschitz bound, L) rose from 3.6 to 69, the read overestimated its own surface by 1-6 mm near it, walls appeared from a
stroke's second crossing, and terraces and fragments by 30 strokes. Nothing kept the stored values true distances, and
the "Protect surfaces behind" rule restored stale values.

**The fix** (`02ab2cfa`, 2026-11-14 02:50 PT): a truncated distance band. On every batch, the stored values within two
band widths of the brush take part; the surface's own nodes stay (the surface does not move); a node whose nearest
surface may have moved takes the converged distance from an eikonal solve; every value is clamped to plus or minus the
band width W = 12 lattice cells. Protect-behind restores the smaller of the stroke-start and current magnitudes, not the
stroke-start value. A second fix (`0e0dbf02`, 06:28 PT) keeps a surface node frozen only while its value can still be a
distance, which removed the walls left by strokes piled on one spot.

| Case (scripted, on the card) | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| The investigation's cases (click, drag, cross, cross twice, Smooth after, silhouette, brick face) | terraces, walls, fragments | 0 walls, 0 fragments; error near the surface 0.25 mm or less; gradient magnitude 1.38 or less |
| 100 Clay and Ctrl-Clay strokes over a front | | 0 walls, 0 holes; a flat 40-44 fps; release 0.15-0.18 s |
| 10 strokes piled on one spot | up to 7,233 walls | 0 |
| 30 overlapping strokes | 1,541-4,077 walls | 0 |
| A mound after Smooth at radius 50 | 1,541 walls, 6 specks, 14 holes | 0, 0, 0 |
| The window's longest wait during a layer Smooth | 117-437 ms (the 2026-11-13 hands-on session logged 14 stalls of 2-2.7 s) | 46-65 ms |

**Open:** by eye, a few small marks remain at a crease root after a large Smooth; a large Smooth leaves a frayed tip
where the coarse level is stale (by about 80 mm, per the run record); after undo, the card's values can sit off the
band (this predates the fix). The band method is the standard narrow-band level-set practice of 1988-1999.

**Evidence state:** measured on named cases.

### 2.4 Undo, save and reopen
- Every toolset command now journals its undo, so undo goes back through it after Save and Open (`34119007`,
  `27dabcba`, `45e70563`). Measured on a save ending in a Merge after a masked twist, a Duplicate, a Mask and a warp:
  journal 7 of 7 steps; after Open, undo exact at every step and redo exact back.
- **Undo all, redo all** (`4fa79c9a`, 12:03 PT). A real bug: a Cut's redo makes its pieces anew (new objects with the
  same ids), and Merge's redo removed its sources by identity, so the history stopped one step short. Fixed. On the CPU
  the milestone's 13 steps undo to the start and redo to the end, exactly, twice over; in the real window on the card
  the adopted and the reopened milestone both return to the start state. A live card session of 13 strokes round-trips
  exactly on the CPU state hash. **The card's own held rows do not hash-equal after the round trip:** the measured
  difference is at most 7.6 um in field value and 2/255 in the picture. It appears representational; it is
  recorded as open until the card's hash is either made equal or shown not to matter.

### 2.5 The window (tier 0 of the ladder)
Built on 2026-11-13/14 (`63ebcd53` and following), as the approved interface design describes:
- **A launch guard** on every card launch of the window's renderer: each pixel pass is cut into tiles predicted at
  about 100 ms (capped at 250 ms by the worst cost a pixel has ever measured), at most two in flight; a launch past 1 s
  stops the call, with no whole-frame fallback. Measured: a 960 x 540 head view's longest launch 4.6 ms; a converged 4K
  still of 16 passes in 1.39 s over 203 launches, longest 10.5 ms. Overnight the longest launch seen was 10-23 ms
  (UNVERIFIED as a range; stated in an overnight summary).
- **A clean exit rule:** stop the guard, join the engine thread (it starts nothing new once stopping), join the hair's
  card threads, synchronise the device, record the exit. An overnight summary reports a clean exit four ways, all exit 0.
- **A neutral studio** built from a formula (no image and no licence) as the default environment.
- **Views:** Clay, Look and Silhouette; an X-ray slider and per-object opacity, composited as real transparency front
  to back; Section with the cut face; select inside (click again to go deeper, A+click to come back), with a covered
  object's outline drawn dashed through what covers it.
- **The approved layout:** the four categories in bold (Add Objects, Manipulate, Building Tools, Corrective), a tool
  strip above the view with fixed slots and the Reach setting, a view bar, edge panels that pin, pop out and dock to any
  edge, a top bar, nothing clipped at 1366 px wide; a transform strip (Move W, Rotate R, Scale Y); keys P, X, K, M, I, T
  and Shift+A for the main tools.
- **A safe test runner** that never imports a test, runs CPU tests with the card hidden and card tests through the
  machine's GPU queue under a memory guard.

**Evidence state:** implemented, with the launch guard measured on named cases. The 11:02 PT hands-on test found the
layout not yet finished (section 3).

### 2.6 Materials: tiers 1 and 2
Landed 2026-11-14 01:57 PT (`ee5db02b`) and extended through 25 updates by 09:22 PT.
- **Structure in the field, not paint:** stone relief, edge chips and cracks are written into the field by one program
  operation; card and CPU agree to 1.0e-7 m in single precision.
- **The look** on the card: a volumetric composition field (grains with their own hues, clear quartz, interstices,
  laminae, rust spots) that keeps the measured mean colour at every zoom; GGX with Schlick's Fresnel and an Oren-Nayar
  diffuse term; grain relief marched at render time, with grain-scale sun shadow and sky occlusion.
- **Measured inputs:** colours from about 95 spectra of public spectral libraries (ECOSTRESS, USGS); warm building
  sandstones from published CIELAB measurements (CC BY 4.0 sources), converted to linear albedo; a new fired-clay brick
  profile (density 1,636 kg/m3, water absorption 14 %, measured values from the profile's sources).
- **Tier 1** (scripted, in the real window): the ladder's standard 215 x 102.5 x 65 mm brick in sandstone, its weight
  from the material (3.30 kg). **Tier 2:** five varied bricks of one stone by Array with Vary; a crack open 34.2 mm of 65 mm in the exact
  section (UNVERIFIED as a figure: stated in an overnight summary; the run record was not located).
- **Later updates:** iron staining built in the volume (patches, Liesegang rings), chips that break the arris and show a
  paler body, a fired skin on brick, weathering as a state (0-100 %), a deposit by exposure (sheltered or in the rain,
  with run-off), granite as crystals by mineral, bedding (natural, face or edge), tooled ("droved") finishes.
- **Short of the references** (a side-by-side comparison against reference photographs): still cooler and flatter than the
  photographs, granite flatter than real, chip bites too smooth in close-up.

**Evidence state:** measured on named cases (the lanes' scripted pass). By the ladder's own rule a tier passes only

## 4. Research direction

### 4.1 The tier ladder: acceptance as scenarios run by hand
Approved 2026-11-14 ~00:23 PT. Each tier is passed first by a lane, scripted through the real window's input from at
least three views (side, three-quarter, above), recorded for exact replay and sent as a numbered sheet beside the
reference; then it is run live by hand with the render on. Throughout: no stall over 100 ms, strokes at 30 fps or
more, nothing changing after a release that was not visible on screen, a clean exit, no driver reset. The three-view
rule is the first release's lesson: its scripted sphere-to-head test passed from two views while a head still could not
be blocked out by hand.

| Tier | Scenario | Pass mark (judged by eye first, then the numbers) | State today |

The ladder is the plan's order: the toolset's core first (one warp engine serving Shape, Transform, Place and the rig),
then the materials needs of tiers 1-2, then one missing piece of physics for tier 3, then the fluids for tier 4.

### 4.2 The skeleton and rig (designed, nothing built)
The question: build an anatomically correct musculo-skeletal system, or simulate it. Decided: **build the skeleton anatomically correct, simulate the soft tissue, and build individual muscles only
where a motion shows the simulation can't produce what a real body does.**
- **Bones** are rigid field parts inside the body, with true lengths and joint centres and axes from measured
  references. Every motion failure so far traced to a wrong bone or joint (a square elbow hinge, a misplaced joint
  centre, a broken thumb chain), never to a missing muscle; a wrong axis moves the wrist tens of millimetres at full
  flexion whatever the tissue does.
- **Joints** are axis lines, applied from the body outward, with ranges counted from anatomical zero and couplings built
  in (shoulder-blade rhythm, forearm rotation, knee screw-home, the jaw's hinge then glide). Ligaments and capsules are
  carried as limits and couplings.
- **Muscle mass, fat and skin** are one layered tissue under the contact engine's one law with a material per layer,
  never solved region by region (a rule since 2026-11-12). Individual muscles come later, one at a time,
  where an active change shows (a flexed biceps, the shoulder girdle overhead, a clenched jaw).
- **The flesh follows the bones through the same one-to-one warp engine as the sculpt tools**, with influence weights
  diffused through the body's material (never across a gap), so nobody paints weights.
- **Two speeds:** the warp alone as a preview while dragging, in milliseconds on the card; the tissue law as a settle on
  release, in the background, and at render time for animation frames. The reason is measured: the live law converged
  an elbow drag at 1.8-26 s a frame on the CPU, and a fist closed by contact (all four finger pads on the palm, gaps
  0.02-0.1 mm) took 37 CPU minutes.
- **Proving poses** (decided): the elbow bent to about 150 degrees with the forearm on the biceps, a fist, a hand to
  the cheek, an open mouth, and a blink. The overhead arm comes after these hold. The elbow target (145-155 degrees) has
  not been reached in any experiment so far.
- **Export** of the skeleton to an open physics format: MJCF first (it carries signed-distance-field collision shapes
  through plugins, so the bones leave as fields), USD Physics second, URDF last.

**Evidence state:** proposed.

### 4.3 Learned motion through open libraries
A survey of open libraries and motion data under one rule: no licence that limits commercial use, in anything shipped
or depended on. Every licence was read from the project's own licence file, README, package metadata or terms page.
- **Simulator:** MuJoCo (Apache-2.0) on the CPU; MuJoCo Warp or Newton (Apache-2.0) for many copies on the card.
- **Imitation learning:** MimicKit (Apache-2.0); ready tracking tasks in mjlab and MuJoCo Playground (Apache-2.0);
  RSL-RL (BSD-3), Stable-Baselines3, CleanRL, TorchRL, skrl (MIT); Gymnasium (MIT) as the interface.
- **Retargeting:** GMR (MIT), on its BVH and FBX paths only.
- **Data we may use:** CMU motion capture (inclusion in commercial products allowed, resale of the data not), 100STYLE
  (CC BY 4.0), ACCAD Open Motion (CC BY 3.0), MoCapAct (CDLA-Permissive-2.0).
- **Out, from their own terms:** SMPL, SMPL-X, AMASS and everything built on them; LAFAN1 and other non-commercial sets;
  Mixamo's content for training; Isaac Gym; Isaac Sim (redistribution, and 16 GB of card memory). Conditional: NVIDIA
  Warp's published wheel carries a component licensed against competing products, so a from-source build without it is
  the clean route for released software (which features that loses is UNVERIFIED).
- **The plan:** after the rig, export the skeleton to MJCF, retarget one CMU clip with GMR, and imitate it with a plain
  tracking-only learner (one clip, a tracking reward, no adversarial discriminator, no latent encoder), on this card in
  the queue. Estimate (not measured): one humanoid fits a thousand or more parallel copies in under 8 GB, enough for
  single-clip imitation, not for hours of motion.

**Evidence state:** proposed (a desk study).

### 4.4 Materials as physical structure
A material is a profile with physical properties (density, friction, stiffness, roughness by finish, measured colour)
and a seeded generator that builds its structure in the field and its optical medium in the renderer: grain, laminae,
iron staining in the volume, chips that expose a paler body, cracks with depth, weathering and deposits as states of
the material. The same idea extends to living tissue: the design of 2026-11-14 is one generator per material,
whose parts are sub-generators under one seed, each with its own window of options. The first example is an eyeball
generator (sclera, cornea, limbus, iris, pupil, lens fitted to the selected form) with the existing seeded iris
generator as a window inside it, meant for a workflow in which two eye sockets are cut from a head with a mirrored
sphere, the two cut volumes shrunk by a fraction of a millimetre, turned into eyes by the generator with slight
per-eye variation, and put back exactly where they were cut from. None of that workflow exists yet: mirrored Cut away,
Shrink / Grow by a distance and an exact put-back are on the toolset lane's list; the generator is the materials lane's,
which is paused.

**Evidence state:** measured on named cases for stone and brick (section 2.6); proposed for the eyeball generator.

### 4.6 Contact and motion (paused by decision)
The contact and motion lanes ran through 2026-11-13 and paused at clean hand-offs so that the tool's baseline comes
first. Their main results since the first release, briefly:
- The elbow's stall near 85-90 degrees: on 2026-11-12/13 the projected Newton step was measured to be about 12 times
  stiffer than the energy along the forming crease, and a kink in the bent elbow was traced to a continuity gap across
  cell faces in the volume map, a finite-difference gradient on the rest field and a hard neighbourhood switch. The
  145-155 degree target is not reached.
- The author's hard-stop contact law (zero force while apart, never crossing, force only where surfaces meet) was
  shown to hold as a law in a constraint form with multipliers, slower to converge than the barrier it was compared with
  (1.4-3.4x the iterations); the concept of warm-starting from stored solves of the same law cut iterations from 351 to 99
  on the test bend. Applying one stored state as it is failed at 2.5-degree spacing.
- A fist closed by contact on the human test model's hand, all four pads reaching the palm.

**Evidence state:** measured on named cases; nothing here is in Field Studio's window.

unchanged from the first release and remains a concept. The tests in 4.5 are the first measured pieces: lazily advanced
state was correct but not yet cheap; the environment field as a broad phase and conservative advancement were both

## 5. Findings since the first release
Each finding: what was seen, the cause, the fix, and how it was checked.

**5.1 The field stopped being a distance under editing.** Seen as terraces, walls and fragments in the 2026-11-13
hands-on session. Cause: nothing kept the stored values true distances after a stroke, and the protect rule restored stale
values. Fix: a truncated distance band (section 2.2). Checked on the investigation's cases and piled-stroke cases.

**5.2 A stale compiled kernel on the card.** For 43 minutes (2026-11-14 05:03-05:46 PT) the card ran an old brush rule.
Cause: the GPU array library caches a compiled module, in memory and on disk across processes, keyed by the main
source text and the compile options only, so an edit to an included header never recompiled. Fix: the compile key
carries a hash of every included header (`5d7a938a`, `5ac3ae51`); a rule for every lane; affected results re-verified
on a fresh compile. The same trap had appeared earlier with the CPU compiler's cache on 2026-11-08 (a cached function
recompiles only when its own file changes).

**5.3 A false recess where two objects touch, in the Section view.** Two separate forms with coincident faces showed a
notch under the section plane. Cause: a section ray tested its starting point on the plane (the cut face) only after a
miss. Fix: test the start on the plane first (`5ab77a44`). In the normal view the dark line at a contact is real
shadow, not a gap; checked and left as it is.

**5.4 Merge left an internal crack.** The exact union of two touching pieces kept each piece's contact face inside the
merged solid, drawn as a line across the old cut. Fix: Merge seals the contact, giving the nodes the contact decides
their distance to the real surface (`45e70563`).

**5.5 The sandstone looked grey; the colour pipeline was not the cause.** A trace of one material end to end
(spectrum, linear albedo, the renderer's input, the displayed colour) on a flat patch under a neutral light showed the
renderer carries the measured chromaticity intact (red/green 1.07 against 1.08, green/blue 1.21 against 1.22). The grey
was the measured specimen itself (a cool, low-chroma laboratory sample). Fix at the cause: measured warm building
sandstones from published CIELAB values (chroma 9-17.5 against the sample's 8.6) added as cited varieties. The display
transform's compression of saturation is noted; comparisons are made through the same display path.

**5.6 A slowdown from 31 to 3 fps** at about 07:00 PT, with two causes: the Materials panel ran a measuring job on every
stroke (`dad67ffd`), and the launch guard recorded host stalls as card time, so one bad reading slowed every later
session (`42bf9255`). After the fixes a still converged in 1.2 s again instead of 6.5 s, and orbit rose from 18 to 39 fps.

**5.7 The redo bug after Cut and Merge** (section 2.4): identity versus id of re-created objects.

**5.8 A panel that failed silently** (section 3, item 3): the fix was in the code; the gap was in how the work was
checked, which is why the landing bar exists.

**5.9 The mid-drag tears are in the live view, not the field** (section 3, item 9): a matrix of cases separated the
render from the field before any fix was written.

**5.10 Display-driver resets came from whole-frame fallbacks.** Three resets on 2026-11-13 (18:25-18:38 PT) during a
blink sequence in the paused offline video pipeline. Cause: when a tiled launch failed, the renderer fell back to a
whole-frame launch and remembered that choice; one plan was also reused across a jump in cost. Fix: a per-launch guard
in that renderer (whole frames refused unless explicitly asked for, no fallback, a launch past 1 s stops the frame;
`913c3c60`) and a guard on its light cache's fill (worst launch 163-195 ms against the old fill's 1,611 ms; `eeed69a9`).
The window's own guard (section 2.5) follows the same rule.

**5.11 The request ledger was recording suggested replies as real requests.** The session application offers a
predicted next message; about 20 of these had reached the project's request inbox looking like genuine requests.
Fixed (`0aad9068`), and the two records they had touched were corrected.

## 6. Tooling
- **The shared-machine GPU queue** (unchanged since its eighth version, 2026-11-06): one card job at a time; a lane keeps
  the card through short review gaps under a 20-minute lease; jobs start in the background and are waited on in short
  calls so a tool timeout can't drop a ticket. Overnight it carried four lanes and a hands-on window on one card;
  waits of ten minutes or more happen under leases.
- **The memory guard:** a heavy job runs inside a Windows job object with its exact peak memory recorded, an optional
  hard cap, and a stop when free RAM stays low; a stop ends only the process it started.
- **The launch guard and the exit rule** (section 2.5), now in the window, the offline renderer and the light cache's
  fill.
- **Compile caches keyed on everything compiled** (section 5.2).
- **Test harnesses:** the milestone session (section 2.1); a deterministic field-health regression that counts walls,
  fragments, holes, specks and the gradient bound on scripted strokes (section 2.2); the safe test runner; per-tool CPU
  references that the card versions are compared against to microns.
- **The landing bar** (since 2026-11-14 11:48 PT): before anything lands in the window, the field core, the card's
  sculpt or trace code, the toolset or the materials code, (1) the real window must build every panel with no "not
  installed" line, and (2) the milestone's CPU run must pass. Both are stated in the landing record; the window lane
  re-runs the card milestone after each block.
- **Records and lessons:** every block of work is recorded the same day with its measured cost; a search tool ranks
  lessons, the build log and the READMEs by words, and an index regenerated from each file's own first lines lists every
  engine module and every research record, plan and design, so new work starts from what exists. A request ledger keeps
  every request made and every run's outcome.

## 7. Methodologies
- **Outcome first.** The target outcome is defined from the references with precision, holding in every view, light,
  zoom and motion it will be seen in, and built directly; causes drive its variation, states and features. Where a
  lane's outcome genuinely needs causes first (behaviour, contact, motion over time), it says so and shows why.
- **Built, not painted.** A look comes from building the structure that produces it (form, volume, translucent layers,
  the optical medium, motion), so it holds when light, view, zoom or motion change. The stone in section 2.6 is built
  this way: its crack is a cavity with depth, its staining is in the volume, its chips cut the form.
- **Fix the named problem at its cause.** When a problem is named, the work finds what causes it and fixes it
  there, not the surroundings: the terraces at the field's distance property, the grey stone at the specimen, the
  Materials crash at its loop variable and then at the checking gap that let it through.
- **Measure before asserting.** Numbers carry their conditions; pass marks are declared before a run; a verdict is by
  the declared marks even when the substance reads otherwise, and both are reported; pictures are looked at before a
  result counts.
- **Use by hand is the yardstick.** Scripted passes come first and are necessary; they are not sufficient.
  A passing milestone is a moment, not a state.
- **The patent standard: proportionate and evidence-based.** A flag justifies a redesign only with the particular claim,
  jurisdiction and status; every required limitation mapped to the actual implementation; the strongest
  counterarguments; and the precise limitation the redesign removes, with its cost. An incomplete mapping is an open
  question, neither a prohibition nor a clearance. Working directions are not stripped because a related patent
  exists. No patented method is meant to ship; patented work is studied and bettered. These are engineering readings by
  non-lawyers; no patent attorney has been consulted, and nothing here is legal advice.
- **Lane ownership on one shared machine.** One dedicated AI coding session owns each area of work and works it
  continuously; a coordinating session plans, briefs, arbitrates and reports but does not carry lane work. A lane
  changing a file another lane owns records why first in a shared coordination log and gets the owner's agreement;
  shared code is built and regressed in a copy before it lands. The card, the memory and the time are shared through
  the queue and guards above. Sequencing between lanes is set by the coordinating session.
